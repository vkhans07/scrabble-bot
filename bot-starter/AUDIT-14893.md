## Exact Z decision

Auction 804069, with rack AAGNPT and 45 coins:

1. Cached estimate submitted bid 34.
2. A 300-scenario fast update estimated a free Z's marginal score at 20.0323
   after smoothing, and cash value lambda at 0.4024.
3. The rule computed floor(20.0323 / 0.4024) = 49, capped to the balance: bid 45.
4. Both bids were acknowledged in about 100–111 ms. The revised bid won;
   runner-up price was 37. The original bid of 34 would have lost.
5. After acquisition, the engine recommended ZAG for 13 points.

Immediate exact disjoint-word value was 16 before buying Z (PAGAN) and 22
with Z (PANT + ZAG). This six-point immediate increment is not itself a fair
bid: future-word and funding effects also matter.

## Structural model error exposed by replay

The native tile arm adds the letter without subtracting an acquisition price.
A separate +15-coins arm estimates cash value, and the live policy divides tile
value by this local cash derivative. This can be a poor approximation for a large
purchase that consumes most of the balance. The +15-coins experiment at balance
45 does not measure the cost of reducing that balance by 37.

A deterministic 2,000-scenario replay used the recorded pre-Z rack, unseen pool,
value table, lambda 0.4494, seed 241024, five Real opponent models, and balances
100, 74, 92, 87, 73. The continuation and scenario-generation functions were
unchanged from those used in this match. Results (future score, excluding points
already earned):

| Arm | Expected future score |
|---|---:|
| Pass: keep rack and 45 coins | 34.042 |
| Receive Z for free: keep 45 coins | 58.861 |
| Buy Z for 37: keep 8 coins | 22.089 |

Within this approximate model, paying 37 for Z loses 11.954 expected future
points versus passing. This is evidence of inconsistent willingness-to-pay
calculation, not an estimate of the real match result if we had passed. The
replay treats the five custom-named opponents as Real, matching the intended
model; per-opponent isSimulated flags were not included in the saved match log.
Future opponent balances and the continuation policy remain approximate.

## Did timing cause this loss?

Eight bid updates were blocked by the old cutoff. Checking the final auction
archive shows that none would have turned a lost auction into a clear win at
that submitted amount. Updates on V, A, N were for auctions we already won;
updates on I, L and R remained below the recorded top bids. This does not prove
that timing has no effect generally, but it does not explain this match's main
budget collapse. All submitted bids were acknowledged; no late rejection occurred.

## Next correction to evaluate

Directly compare paid acquisition with passing: both add the letter and subtract
the candidate price inside the rollout. For second-price bids, estimate marginal
willingness to pay while preserving the opportunity cost of the depleted budget;
include the opposing-price distribution when evaluating the actual bidding rule.
Test this against the large-purchase replay before a controlled live comparison.
Increasing sample count alone cannot correct the free-letter/paid-letter mismatch.

No strategy change was deployed during the match audit itself. In a follow-up,
the native current-letter calculation was changed to simulate adding the tile
and subtracting several candidate runner-up prices. It estimates the highest
price with positive expected benefit and uses that as the second-price bid cap.
The continuous bot was restarted with this revision after match 14994; transition
match 15004 is excluded from before/after performance comparisons. The live bot
remains running. Source logs: `logs/continuous-20261024-provided/match-14893.jsonl`.
